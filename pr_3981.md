## What a document does now on Letter

- A US Letter document has a window convention for the US #10 envelope, written once as a function of the paper (`AddressWindow.fieldOn(paper)` in `deskilo_document_model`): recipient field left 25.4, top 56, 83 x 21 mm; the sender owns everything above 50 mm; nothing but the recipient between 50 and 93 mm; the flow resumes at 93 mm; footer and continuation pages as on A4, from the Letter page's own height.
- `left` and `right` both mean this one window on Letter; `off` stays off. A design keeps the kind it chose and prints correctly on either paper.
- The banded engine places the recipient in the Letter field (it no longer switches the window off; `addressWindowOnPaper` is gone). The positioned engine uses the Letter field for a named window (`placedOffEnvelope` is gone, `onPaper` keeps the recipient). A design with an explicit recipient frame is not touched.
- `reportZones(window, paper:)` returns the Letter zones. `tool/report.dart check` prints them, measures the band between the sender and the flow across the page on Letter, reads the footer limits from the paper (the 235, 240 and 297 mm of A4 are no longer written in it), and reports a recipient whose lines do not all fit the field.
- The comment that said 85 x 45 mm now says 85 x 40 mm, as the constant and the rules do.
- Docs: the window-envelope section of `docs/AGENT_RULES.md` carries the Letter convention with the stock it is measured against, and its pointer to where the constants live is corrected; the guide's sentence on Letter names the envelope and says to seat the letter at the bottom of it, in the five languages (chapter sources, assembled wiki guides, `assets/help` and the guide site regenerated with the repository's tools).

## What stays exactly as it was on A4

Every A4 value is the one it was (20 / 110 mm left, 45, 85 x 40, flow at 90, type 11 pt). No A4 golden changed; the only goldens regenerated are the two Letter ink goldens (`invoice_ink_banded_letter.txt`, `invoice_ink_positioned_letter.txt`), because a Letter document is meant to change. A shipped layout's `<body y="90mm">` is read as before on A4.

## Decisions the specification did not give

- The recipient's type size is part of the field: 11 pt on A4 (unchanged), 9.5 pt on Letter. At 11 pt five lines (the name and four lines of address) do not fit 21 mm and the engine drops the whole address text; at 9.5 pt five lines end at 75.5 mm and a sixth does not fit.
- A recipient text that does not fit is not drawn by the engine, so "reported, not printed outside" is done in the check: it compares the lines of `client_name` (or `member`) and `client_address` with the lines found inside the field and names the ones that do not fit.
- A body stated as exactly `90mm` under a named window, on a Letter design, follows the paper's resume line (93 mm). The shipped letter layouts and the sample state `y="90mm"`; without this they would print ink at 90 to 93 mm on Letter. On A4 nothing is read differently. A design that states another `y` keeps it.
- The shipped layouts' header height (25 mm) is kept on Letter; the sender's limit (50 mm) governs a header without an explicit height.
- On Letter the check's band rule is the whole band 50 to 93 mm across the page outside the field; on A4 it stays the band under the aperture, left of 100 mm, as before.
