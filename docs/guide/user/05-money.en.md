### Read your statement

**Audience:** Member

You want to know where the month stands: what you used, what it costs and what is left to settle.

<p><img src="images/user-money-statement--top.en.jpg" width="280"></p>

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money) and stay on **Statement**.
2. Use the arrows beside the month name to browse other months.
3. Read **Balance** first: **Outstanding** in red means you owe it, **Settled** means nothing is left.
4. Read **This month** below it: the days you used out of the days your plan includes, and the days left.
5. Read the cards that follow: your subscription, extra half-days, services, packages, open positions and **Payments & credits**.

**Good to know**

- A booked morning or afternoon counts as half a day, so you may see values like 0.5 days.
- The card also states your plan's rule. With pay as you go it always shows the rate of extra days; with the other two rules it tells you to ask an administrator or to buy a package once all your days are used.
- A line marked "pending validation" is waiting for someone to confirm it and is not counted yet.
- Tap the PDF icon beside the month to export the bill.

**See also:** [Pay what you owe](help:user.money.payments) · [What each booking cost](help:user.money.usage)

### When a month has been invoiced

**Audience:** Member

You want to know which figure to trust once the workspace has sent you an invoice for a month.

<p><img src="images/user-money-statement-invoiced--card.en.jpg" width="280"></p>

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money) and go back to the invoiced month with the left arrow.
2. Look for the card named **Invoice** (or **Credit note** when the total is negative) followed by its number.
3. Read the state on the card, then **Invoice total**; a partly paid invoice also shows **Paid so far** and **Remaining to pay**.

**Good to know**

- Once a month is invoiced, the invoice decides whether it is settled. The payment that clears it usually arrives in a later month, so the month's own balance is no longer the reference.
- A credit note reads "The workspace owes you this amount": there is nothing to pay on your side.

**See also:** [Your invoices](help:user.money.invoices)

<!-- anchor: user.money.payments -->
### Pay what you owe

**Audience:** Member

You want to settle your balance and know how the workspace expects the money.

<p><img src="images/user-money-payments.en.jpg" width="280"></p>

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money) and choose **Payments**.
2. Check **Balance** under **Pay**; overdue invoices from all periods are called out above it.
3. Read **Payment instructions**: the bank details, the payment reference to quote and the other ways the workspace accepts. Tap an IBAN or a value to copy it.
4. If the button is there, tap **Pay online**.
5. Once you have paid another way, [record the payment](help:user.money.payments.record).

**Good to know**

- The instructions appear only while something is owed, and only if the workspace has set them up. If there is nothing, ask your administrator how to pay.
- An online payment the provider has not confirmed shows as **Online payment pending**: the balance keeps showing what is owed until it is confirmed.
- Under **Requests** you can also submit an expense, ask for **Request extra half-days** or, if your plan works with packages, **Buy a package**.

**See also:** [Record a payment](help:user.money.payments.record) · [Submit an expense](help:user.money.expense)

<!-- anchor: user.money.payments.record -->
### Record a payment

**Audience:** Member

You paid by transfer, cash or another way and you want the workspace to know.

<p><img src="images/user-money-payments-record.en.jpg" width="280"></p>

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money), choose **Payments** and tap **Record a payment**.
2. Enter the **Amount**.
3. Tap how you paid: **Bank transfer**, **Cash**, **PayPal**, **TWINT**, **Card**, **Wero**, **Lydia**, **Wise** or **Other**. Tap it again to clear it.
4. Check **Payment date** and **Applies to**, the month this payment settles.
5. Add a **Note (optional)**, then tap **Submit for confirmation**.

**Good to know**

- Your payment is not final when you send it. It waits as "pending validation" until the people the workspace has chosen confirm it, as set in [Validation rules](help:user.validation.overview). Only then does it settle your balance.
- Your payment date cannot be in the future. **Applies to** can reach one month ahead, to pay in advance.

**See also:** [Pay what you owe](help:user.money.payments) · [Finance alerts](help:user.money.alerts)

<!-- anchor: user.money.invoices -->
### Your invoices

**Audience:** Member

You want to find an invoice, see whether it is paid and get its PDF.

<p><img src="images/user-money-invoices.en.jpg" width="280"></p>

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money) and choose **Invoices**.
2. Read the list, newest first. Each row shows the number, a status chip, the month, the date and the amount.
3. Tap an invoice to open it.
4. Tap **Quick view** to read it on screen, **Download PDF** to save it or **Share PDF** to send it.

<p><img src="images/user-money-invoices-detail.en.jpg" width="280"></p>

**Good to know**

- The statuses are **Open**, **Awaiting validation**, **Paid**, **Partially paid**, **Partially paid · remainder cancelled** and **Refunded**. An open invoice says when it is due or how many days it is overdue, and how many reminders you have received.
- Tap the payment icon on an open invoice to go to **Payments**.
- If the list is empty, the workspace has not invoiced you yet; it invoices a month once it closes.
- Invoices cannot be edited. A wrong invoice is marked **Erroneous** and replaced by a new one, and the erroneous one leaves your list. When invoices are regrouped into one, the regrouped invoice replaces them in your list.
- A line invoiced by the hour, the day or a weight (for example *1.5 h at 90 EUR*, or *0.75 kg at 12.5 EUR per 10 kg*) shows under its label the exact quantity, its unit and the price it was entered at, as the PDF prints them; its amount is the one fixed when the invoice was issued. A line recorded by a newer version of the app is shown as written and says it cannot be used here: update the app to work with it.

**See also:** [Pay what you owe](help:user.money.payments) · [Your finances across spaces](help:user.money.finances)

### Submit an expense

**Audience:** Member

You paid something for the space and you want the workspace to reimburse you.

<p><img src="images/user-money-expense.en.jpg" width="280"></p>

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money), choose **Payments** and tap **Submit an expense**.
2. Enter the **Amount**.
3. Pick a **Category**: **Coffee & kitchen**, **Equipment**, **Supplies** or **Other**.
4. Write a **Description**.
5. If you bought something members will use, turn on **This is a supply for the space** (it appears when your workspace has **Supplies from expenses** switched on) and fill in the item, the quantity and the unit price.
6. Tap **Submit for confirmation**.

**Good to know**

- You see "Expense submitted — waiting for approval". The expense counts only once it is confirmed.
- A confirmed supply goes on the shelf as a service: members who use it pay for it.
- In a VAT-registered space with **Input VAT** switched on, choose the **VAT on this expense** and the **Deductible share (%)** — 100 % unless part of the expense is private or serves an exempt activity. Once the expense is approved, its deductible VAT is subtracted on the VAT return of the month it was approved. Turn on **A fixed asset** for equipment the space keeps and depreciates: its VAT is declared on its own line (CA3 line 19). A scheduled expense asks the same once, and every occurrence carries it.
- Recurring costs have their own door, **Scheduled expenses**, next to this button.

**See also:** [Approve or decline an expense](help:user.money.expense.approve)

<!-- anchor: user.money.expenses.vat -->
### The VAT of an expense

**Audience:** Member · Administrator · Owner

An expense carries VAT, and you want the space to deduct the right part of it.

**Steps**

1. In the expense form, or in **Scheduled expenses**, choose **VAT on this expense**: a rate, or **No VAT**.
2. Enter the **Deductible share (%)**: 100 unless part of the expense is private or serves an exempt activity.
3. Turn on **A fixed asset** for equipment the space keeps and depreciates.
4. For a service bought abroad that carries no VAT of your country, turn on **Bought from a supplier abroad**. The amount you typed is then the base: the space owes the acquisition tax on it and deducts its share.

**Good to know**

- The fields appear when the space is VAT-registered and **Input VAT** is switched on.
- **Bought from a supplier abroad** appears only where your country's return has a box for that tax, for example the Swiss return (Ziffer 383).
- A scheduled expense asks once, and every occurrence carries the answer.
- The deductible VAT counts in the month the expense is approved.

**See also:** [Submit an expense](help:user.money.expense) · [The Swiss return: Ziffern 205 and 383](help:user.money.vat.ch-return)

1. Open [Guest passes](app:/money/guest-passes), or **Guest passes** on the [Sales](app:/money/sales) page.

### The Purchases page and Add purchase / expense

**Audience:** Member · Administrator · Owner

You bought something for the space, or a supplier sent a bill, and you want to record it.

<!-- image: user-money-purchases -->

**Steps**

1. Open [Purchases](app:/money/purchases), or tap **Purchases** in [My finances](app:/money).

<!-- anchor: user.money.purchases.bills -->
### A supplier bill, from capture to approval

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

A supplier sent a bill and you want the space to owe it, with a second person's approval.

**Steps**

1. In **Purchases**, tap **Add supplier bill**, or use **Add purchase / expense**.
2. Choose the **Supplier** and enter the **Invoice number**, the lines and the **Total**. A bill saved incomplete stays a draft: "A draft: send it for approval when it is complete."
3. Tap **Send for approval**.
4. Another person opens [Supplier bills to approve](app:/money/purchases/approvals) and taps **Approve**, or **Reject** with the reason.
5. Once approved, the bill is owed to the supplier until it is paid.

**Good to know**

- A bill needs its supplier and an amount before it can be sent.
- The person who sent a bill cannot decide it. In the workspace settings, **Bills one person may approve below (currency units)** lets that person approve small bills, if they may approve expenses. 0 keeps the rule that another person approves every bill. Rejecting always takes another person.
- Each document shows its approval, its settlement (**Unpaid**, **Partly settled**, **Settled**, **Nothing owed**) and its accounting state apart.
- Capturing a bill needs **Capture supplier bills**. Deciding one needs **Approve expenses**.

**See also:** [What the space owes: To pay](help:user.money.purchases.to-pay) · [Supplier balances](help:user.money.suppliers.balance)

<!-- anchor: user.money.purchases.to-pay -->
### What the space owes: To pay

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to see what the space still owes, and record what was paid.

**Steps**

1. In **Purchases**, open the **To pay** tab, or open [Cash](app:/money/cash) and tap **To pay**.
2. Read **Owed to suppliers** for approved bills and **Owed to members** for the reimbursements of approved receipts.
3. Tap **Record payment** on a line. Enter the **Amount paid**, the date (**Paid on**), the **Method** and, if you wish, a **Reference (optional)**.
4. For a member who also owes the space, tap **Offset against an invoice** instead. Choose the **Invoice**: the claim settles that open invoice rather than being paid out.
5. To apply a supplier credit note, tap **Apply to its bill** on the credit note.

**Good to know**

- Record only money that was actually paid. The payment is reported by you; it is not confirmed by the bank.
- A wrong payment is never deleted. Tap **Reverse** and state the reason. A payment already applied to an invoice cannot be reversed.
- An offset keeps the invoice's sales and VAT; only its payment is explained.
- A credit note is applied by another person than the one who captured it, to an approved bill of the same supplier that still owes at least that much.
- In the workspace settings, **Payments a second person records from (currency units)** means a payment of at least that amount cannot be recorded by the person who approved the bill it pays. 0 switches the rule off.
- Recording a payment needs **Record payments to members**; an offset needs **Issue invoices & match payments**.

**See also:** [The Cash page](help:user.money.cash)

<!-- anchor: user.money.purchases.lease -->
### The premises lease

**Audience:** Owner · Administrator with the permission

The space rents its premises, and you want the rent and charges it owes recorded once, with their history.

<!-- image: user-money-purchases-lease -->

**Steps**

1. Open [Purchases](app:/money/purchases) and tap **Premises lease**.
2. Tap **Add a lease**. Choose the **Lessor (a supplier)**, write the **Premises (address)** and the **Starts on** date. Add **Fixed until**, **Notice in months**, the **Deposit** and an **Indexation (note)** if the lease has them.
3. Under **Rent and charges**, tap **New amount**. Choose the **Charge** (**Rent**, **Service charge advance** or **Other charge**), the **Amount**, how often it is **Paid** and **From** which day.
4. When the rent changes, add a **New amount** from its date. It replaces the amount in force; the earlier one stays in the history.
5. To end the lease, tap **Give notice**. Enter **Notice given on**, **The lease ends on** and **Why does it end?**.
6. Once the lessor bills the year's service charges, tap **Settle charges**. Enter the period, the **Amount billed** and the day it is **Due on**.

**Good to know**

- Record the lessor as a supplier first.
- After notice, the scheduled amounts stop at the day the lease ends. Occurrences already presented are kept.
- A settlement deducts the advances paid in the period: a balance is owed on the day you chose, a surplus is recorded as a credit. A period is settled once.
- Recording the lease needs **Record the premises lease**; the space must have **Premises lease** switched on.

**See also:** [Rent commitments and the bills that meet them](help:user.money.purchases.commitments) · [Suppliers](help:user.money.suppliers.balance)

<!-- anchor: user.money.purchases.commitments -->
### Rent commitments and the bills that meet them

**Audience:** Owner · Administrator with the permission

The lease or a rented room expects rent each month, and you want each month matched to the lessor's bill, without counting the cost twice.

**Steps**

1. In **Premises lease** or **Rented rooms**, tap **Commitments**.
2. Read each month: **Expected**, **Partly matched** or **Matched**.
3. Tap **Match a bill** on a month. Choose the **Bill** and the **Line of the bill**, and enter the **Amount covered**.
4. If the bill differs from what the month expects, tap **Accept the difference** and write **Why the bill differs**.
5. A wrong match is undone with **Reverse** and its reason.

**Good to know**

- A bill matched to a month replaces that month's expectation; it never adds a second cost.
- Only the lessor's pending or approved supplier bills can be matched, up to what the month still expects.
- An accepted difference goes to the validators: nothing changes until they decide.

**See also:** [The premises lease](help:user.money.purchases.lease) · [Rented rooms](help:user.money.purchases.rented-rooms)

<!-- anchor: user.money.purchases.rented-rooms -->
### Rented rooms

**Audience:** Owner · Administrator with the permission

The space rents rooms from a lessor, and you want members to book them only while the rental runs, and only the members you choose.

<!-- image: user-money-purchases-rented-rooms -->

**Steps**

1. In the floor plan, mark the floor as rented: under **Floors**, it reads "Rented floor: its rooms are booked only inside a rental".
2. Open [Purchases](app:/money/purchases), tap **Rented rooms**, and tap **Rent this room** on a room.
3. Choose the **Lessor**, the **First day** and, if it ends, the **Last day (optional)**. Enter the **Cost to the space per month** and the **Fee per half day for a member (optional)**.
4. Choose who may book: **Open to every member**, or **Also open to these members**, **Also open to these roles** or **Also open to these plans**, each with its own fee if you wish.
5. Set the **Status** to **Active: bookable** and tap **Save**.

**Good to know**

- A rented room can be booked only inside its rental period, and only by the people you chose. Nobody else sees it.
- While the status is **Draft: not bookable**, nobody can book it. Once the rental **Ended**, the room is not bookable again until a new rental starts.
- The monthly cost appears in **Commitments**, to be matched to the lessor's bills.
- Managing rented rooms needs **Manage sites and edit the floor plan**; the space must have **Rented rooms** switched on.

**See also:** [Rent commitments and the bills that meet them](help:user.money.purchases.commitments)

<!-- anchor: user.money.purchases.equipment -->
### The equipment register

**Audience:** Owner · Administrator with the permission

The space bought long-lasting equipment, and you want it registered and treated as your accountant decides.

<!-- image: user-money-purchases-equipment -->

**Steps**

1. On the purchase, classify the line as long-lasting equipment.
2. Open [Purchases](app:/money/purchases), tap **Equipment** and then **Register equipment**. Choose the **Purchase line** and give the **Name of the equipment**.
3. Tap **Decide treatment**. Choose **Capitalise as equipment** or **Expense**, write the **Basis of the decision**, and add **Available for use on** only if you know it.
4. To keep your accountant's figures, tap **Accountant's valuations** and **Import a file** (or **Type in one asset**). Check the preview and tap **Confirm**.

**Good to know**

- Each piece shows its state: **Waiting for treatment**, **Capitalised, in use**, **Expensed** or **Disposed of**.
- The cost is known once the purchase's deductible VAT share is qualified.
- Valuations are evidence only: nothing is posted from them, and a blank figure stays unknown. A later batch replaces an earlier one and says why.
- Registering needs **Capture supplier bills**; deciding the treatment and the valuations need **Manage tariffs and billing rules** and **View workspace finances**.

**See also:** [A supplier bill, from capture to approval](help:user.money.purchases.bills)

### Quotes

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

Open **Sales & collections** and then **Quotes**. This is where you make an offer to a billing customer before you invoice them.

Choose **New quote**, pick the customer, set the dates and add the lines. **Save draft** keeps it; **Send** freezes that revision, takes the next reference from the quote series and works the amounts out from your own tax settings. A draft shows no amounts: they exist only once it is sent. A sent quote never changes: **Amend** makes another revision, and **Cancel the quote** withdraws it. An offer past its date reads as expired. Nothing is invoiced, booked or paid by a quote; invoicing an accepted one comes next.

Once the customer has answered, choose **Record the answer** on a sent quote: you record their answer as the issuer (accepted or declined, the date, how it reached you); it is your record, not their signature, and an offer past its date can no longer be accepted. For an accepted quote choose **Invoice this quote**, set the dates and **Check the invoice**: the invoice is previewed from the accepted revision and set against what was accepted. If it differs (the date, the customer's details or the tax facts changed) you confirm that you reviewed it; what cannot be issued yet is named. Then **Issue the invoice**: one quote makes one invoice, whatever happens next to that invoice.

How long a new quote stays valid when you state no end date, and the longest validity you accept, are your own settings: **Default validity of a quote** (30 days to begin with) and **Longest validity of a quote** (365 days to begin with), in the workspace settings. A longer validity is refused when the quote is saved.

On a sent quote, **Share the PDF** opens the offer as a document: a quote, with its own reference and how long it is valid, the customer, the lines and what they come to as they were frozen when it was sent (the net, the VAT per rate and the total). It is never an invoice and carries no invoice number. You can view it, save it or share it.

### Stock

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

Open **Stock** in the menu, directly below **Purchases & suppliers**. Everything about items and stock is reached there, and only there is it set up.

- **Stock** shows what is on hand by item, variant, lot and place, with the exact quantity.
- **Movements** is the log of every movement; open one to see what moved, as stored and as entered.
- **Transfers** shows the goods that are on their way between two sites, and is where you move goods, receive what arrived and take goods out.
- **Items** is the item registry: each item's stock settings, its variants and its supplier articles.
- **Places** holds the sites, zones, shelves and bins with their addresses.
- **Stock settings** keeps every stock parameter on one page: the stock features with their switches, and the stock rules. A stock setting you find elsewhere links here.

**Moving goods.** On **Transfers**, **Move goods** takes an item from one place to another. Two places of the same site move the goods at once; places of different sites load them onto the way, and they stay on the way, visible on **Transfers**, until the other site receives them. **Receive** on a load takes all or part of it onto a place of the destination site; what you leave out stays on the way. A quantity is exact as you type it, and a request that asks for more than is on hand is refused whole: nothing moves.

**Taking goods out.** When the feature *Take out goods* is on and your role may move stock, **Take out goods** records goods used by the business or lost, and they leave the stock for good.

A sale that cannot be confirmed because the main store holds less than it asks for says what the store holds and offers **Open the stock** for that item.

An entry appears only when its feature is on and your role allows its page: someone who may only look sees the pages, not the settings. Purchases keeps what is bought; Sales keeps what is sold.

### Places and addresses

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to say where things are kept, so that receiving, counting and picking know where to go.

Open **Stock → Places**. A place has a level: **Site** (a branch or a central depot), **Zone** (a sales floor, a back room, a yard), **Aisle**, **Bay** and **Shelf** or **Bin**. A place sits only under the level directly above it: a zone under a site, an aisle under a zone, a bay under an aisle, a shelf under a bay. A counter, a kitchen, a vehicle, a machine or a slot is placed anywhere. A warehouse made before the levels counts as a site.

Each place has an **address**, written from its parents' codes: `MAIN-SALES-A03-B02-S4` is the main store, the sales floor, aisle 3, bay 2, shelf 4. The code of a place only has to differ from its neighbours under the same parent, so every bay can have its shelf `S4`. Rename a place and the addresses below it follow. Type any part of an address, a name or a barcode in the search to find a place. A place is read by its address wherever it appears: in the stock, the movements, a transfer's route and the places a move offers. **Print labels** makes a PDF of the places shown, one label each: the address in large type, the name beneath and a barcode (the place's own barcode, else its address) that a scan finds again.

**Good to know**
- A site says whether it **receives from suppliers**; switch it off and goods arrive there only by transfer from another site.
- A place below a site is **picking** (front of house) or **reserve** (overstock); it is advice, never a quantity.
- The character between the levels is the business's setting **Separator in a place address** (a dash, a dot, a slash or a colon); only how an address is written changes. It is set in **Stock settings**.

### Reorder: what is low and what to order

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to see what is about to run out and order it without going through the shelves.

When an item falls to its reorder point, whoever may manage the stock or the purchases gets **one notice** in the inbox (under Money): the item, the site, what is on hand and the point. It is told once while the stock stays low, and says so when the stock rises above the point again. Tapping it opens this page.

Open **Stock → Reorder** (the feature *Reorder points* must be on). The page has two lists.

- **Low stock** lists every item or variant at or below its reorder point, per site, with what is on hand and the point. It is read from the stock ledger, exactly, in the item's unit; an item with no reorder point is never listed. A place that is in transit does not count as stock on hand.
- **What to order** groups what is low by the supplier whose article is marked **preferred** for it, in that article's purchase unit, rounded up to whole units. An item with no preferred supplier article is listed under **No supplier chosen**: mark one in the item's supplier articles.

**Create draft order** makes a draft purchase order from a group. It is only a draft: nothing is ordered, priced or sent until you confirm it in the purchase orders. It needs the right to manage purchases; asking twice for the same draft makes it once.

**Reorder points** (below the two lists) are what makes an item low. Each shows the item, the site, the level and what to order then: a quantity, *up to* a level, or nothing (it only flags). Whoever may manage the stock adds one with **Add a reorder point** or opens one to change it. The level and the quantity are exact numbers (a decimal comma is fine); a level to order up to must be above the point, a point names a quantity or a level, not both, and an item has one point per site (and variant). The item, variant and site of a point cannot be changed afterwards: make another. A point switched off is kept and flags nothing.

Open **Stock → Items**. Each line shows the **barcode**, the **unit**, the **price** (the catalogue sale price), the **last known cost**, the **home place** and what is **on hand**. ### Item groups

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to say once what a family of items has in common — how long it keeps, how it is tracked, how often it is counted — and have every item follow.

Open **Stock → Item groups** (it needs the *Item groups* feature under stock management). A group has a name, sits at the top or inside another group (one level of sub-groups, no more) and carries the settings you choose: **tracking**, **shelf life**, **tag mode**, **inventory unit**, **count every n days**, **minimum age** and **sold by weight**. A setting you leave *Not set* is not decided at this level.

- **An item follows its group unless it keeps its own value.** The value in force is the item's own if it has one, else its sub-group's, else its group's. Open an item's **Group and inherited settings** (the folder icon on its line in **Stock → Items**) to see where each setting comes from (*From Drinks since 3 Dec* or *Own value*), to move it into another group, and to give an own value back with **Use the group's value**. The item's own **Stock settings** (tracking, shelf life, inventory unit) say *Own value* beside a setting where the item keeps a value of its own against its group.

Nothing is applied until you save a second time; editing anything in between asks for a new preview. An item the change cannot reach (for example a unit on an item that already has movements) is listed, and the group's change still saves for the others.
- **Proposals and prices.** A group can propose to a new item a **default purchase unit** with its factor (a number or a fraction such as 1/3), a **reorder point and quantity** and a **home zone**: they are copied into a new item as a starting point and reach no existing item. **VAT rate**, **supply class**, **price rounding** and **target margin on cost** are shown and set only by someone who may manage services and see the finances; for anyone else they are neither shown nor changed by a save.
- **History.** The group's sheet lists every change with how many items it reached; an item's source line says since when.
- A sub-group cannot have sub-groups; per-piece tags need tracking by piece; a group still used by items cannot be deleted (move them first). Facts already recorded — invoices, receipts, movements — never change.
- Changing groups needs the right to manage the stock; reading them needs the right to see it.

### Working with a scanner

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to receive, move and take out goods by scanning, without touching a field.

A barcode scanner in keyboard mode (it types the code and presses Enter) works in the move and take-out sheets, in the put-away of a delivery, and on **Stock → Items** and **Stock → Places**. You do not tap a field first. A text field that has the focus keeps its own typing, so what you type is never taken for a scan.

- **Scan the place, then the items.** A place code sets the current place; it stays until you scan another and is shown at the top (*Placing into …*). In a move, the first place is where the goods come from and the second where they go. An item, variant or package code names the item and counts it: a carton code adds the carton's exact quantity (for example 12 pieces), and a lot label sets the lot.
- **A repeated scan** adds one more, or, when your business chose so in the stock settings (*Repeated scan*), puts you on the quantity field instead.
- **A code that names nothing** says *Unknown code … — add it to an item?*; whoever may manage the stock can open the items and give it to one. A place that cannot take goods, or an item that is not kept in stock, is refused in words and nothing is filled.
- **Nothing is sent by a scan.** It only fills the form; the button you press runs the same command, with the same rights, as without a scanner.
- **The camera.** On a phone or tablet, **Scan with the camera** (in the scan bar of a goods sheet) opens the camera as a scanner: product barcodes (EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A/E, Code 128) and the 2D labels of places and GS1 products (DataMatrix, QR). Every code goes through the same path as a scanner that types, and the camera stays open for repeated scans, saying how many codes it read and the last line. A barcode whose check digit is wrong is *not read cleanly*: scan again, nothing is sent. A GS1 label sets the item and, where the item tracks lots or expiry, the lot and expiry date. How a read is confirmed (nothing, a vibration, a click) is a device setting in **Stock → Stock settings → Scanner**. If the camera cannot start, allow camera access for DesKilo in the device's settings; in a browser or on a desktop, type the code or use a hardware scanner.
- **Settings.** Whether scanning is used is the business's *Scanner* feature under stock management. The longest gap between two keys of one scan (a person types slower than a scanner) and what ends a code (Enter, Tab or either) belong to the device, in **Stock → Stock settings → Scanner**.

### Tags with the phone's NFC

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to bind an NFC chip to an item or a place, and read chips as you work, with the phone you already have.

With the *Tags* feature on and a phone that reads NFC, **Stock → Items** and **Stock → Places** show a tag button on each line, and the scan bar of a goods sheet shows **Read a tag**. The button is absent on a device without NFC (a desktop, most tablets, a phone with NFC switched off): use a scanner or a handheld reader there.

- **Bind a tag.** Open the tags of an item or a place, tap **Read a tag** and hold the chip to the back of the phone. One read binds one tag. Reading the same chip again says it was already bound here and binds nothing new; a chip that is bound to something else is refused and says to what. An owner may carry several tags. The server takes the code in any case and with spaces, colons or dashes.
- **Retire a tag** that is lost, damaged or replaced: choose the reason. A tag retired lately cannot be bound again at once (the waiting time is a stock setting), and a tag on a piece that is still in stock cannot be retired.
- **Read in a goods sheet.** A chip is read like a scanned code: a place tag sets the current place, an item tag counts the item. A chip read twice in one sheet is ignored (*already read*); a chip that names nothing says *Unknown tag*.
- **UHF labels** cannot be read by a phone. An item that only has them says *use the RFID reader*.
- Binding needs the right to manage the stock; reading needs the right to see it.

### Till

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You serve customers at the counter and want each sale made in seconds, numbered, and paid in cash.

Open **Till** in the sales menu (it needs the counter receipts feature and the right to issue invoices). The items are keys: a tap rings one up, a second tap adds one more. Tap a line to type its **Quantity sold** exactly, for example 0.350 for a weighed item (a comma is read as a point; the quantity must be above zero, with at most six decimals). The server prices the basket, with the tax included, and the till shows **Total to pay**; nothing is priced on the phone.

Press **Take cash** and type the **Cash given** (or **Exact amount**). Less than the total is refused before anything is sent. The sale is numbered by the server and the till says the change due; **Next customer** starts a clean basket. Sending the same sale twice never sells twice.

Where your country does not yet allow the counter, the till says which operation is missing and offers nothing: nothing is numbered and nothing is taken. A goods line needs stock management on; a refusal of the server is shown in words. Card payments, discounts, refunds and returns are not part of this first till.

A traced good names what it was taken from. When you sell an item tracked by lot without choosing a lot, the sale takes it from the lots on hand, the first to expire first and never an expired one; under the sold line the till shows each lot's code as received with the quantity taken from it, for example **Lot SUP-A × 2 · Lot SUP-B × 1**, and each piece by its tag. Where the business uses a scanner, scan a piece's tag to ring up that piece: the sale takes exactly the pieces scanned, and the same piece twice is refused. These track IDs are frozen with the receipt: changing a lot or a tag later never alters a receipt already issued. In the stock, the trace of a lot or a piece names the receipt that sold it.

### RFID tags: one per batch or one per piece

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You decide whether a tag on an item names one batch or one piece, and what a bulk read counts.

With the *Tags* feature on, an item carries a **tag mode**, set on its group and kept by the item unless it has an own value. Open an item's group settings (**Stock → Items**, the folder icon on its line) and read **Tag on this item**: it says where the value comes from (*From Drinks since 3 Dec* or *Own value*), lets you give the item its own value, and **Use the group's value** gives it back.

- **No tags.** The item carries no tag.
- **One tag per batch.** One tag names one lot or one package: a crate, a bucket, a carton. Reading it counts one batch, never one piece.
- **One tag per piece.** One tag names one piece, which needs the item to be tracked by piece. Under **Tags** of the item, **Pieces tagged one by one (n)** lists each piece with its tag.

- A bulk read counts pieces only for per-piece items. For a per-batch item it counts batches and shows the lot's recorded quantity beside the count, never as a counted figure.
- The mode of an item that already has tags cannot change until its tags are retired or its stock is zero; a group's change then skips the item and lists it as kept. A tag on a place always names one place.
- A tag code is a UHF label (EPC, an SGTIN-96 is read as its GTIN and serial by the server) or an NFC chip, stored in upper-case hex and unique in the business across items, pieces and places. Binding and retiring need the right to manage the stock; reading needs the right to see it.

### Handling units

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want goods to travel in the pallet or carton they came in, or in boxes of your own, and still know where every lot and piece is.

With the *Handling units* feature on, open **Stock → Handling units**. A unit has a code of your own (and the supplier's label when it came on a labelled pallet), a kind (pallet, carton, crate, box), a place and a content: items with their lot, quantity and pieces. The list shows the open units, at one place or everywhere.

- **Make a unit.** Press *Make a unit*: its code, its kind, its place and what it holds. A unit claims only stock that is **loose** at that place (what the place holds minus what other units there already hold); asking for more is refused and says how much is loose.
- **Move.** *Move* takes the whole content to another place in one request: every lot and piece keeps its own trace. If someone moved or unpacked the unit since you read it, the move is refused and you read it again.
- **Unpack.** *Unpack* leaves the content where it is, loose, and the unit is no longer open.
- **Repack into boxes.** *Repack into boxes* takes a quantity out of a unit and puts it into boxes of your own (a number of boxes, a quantity in each). What goes in is exactly what came out: the stock total never changes, and a lot followed through the repack is found on both sides. A repack happens at one place; move the new boxes afterwards.

- Stock that is packed in a unit cannot be taken out loose: the server refuses and names the unit. Move or unpack the unit first.
- How an item is traced is chosen per item (or inherited from its group): not traced, by batch (with an expiry date where set) or by piece (a tag per piece).

### The expiry report

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to know, at a date you choose, what in stock has expired and what is about to, and to print it.

With the *Expiry report* feature on, open **Stock → Expiry report**. It lists every lot on hand, and every piece of an item tracked piece by piece, that is **expired** at the reference date (its best-before date is before it) or **expiring** from it, one line each with the item, the lot, the place, the quantity in its unit and how far its date is from the reference date (*6 d past*, *today*, *in 9 d*). The list is sorted by date, then by place; a lot with nothing left on hand is not listed, and a piece shows its tag.

- **Reference date.** Today on the business's calendar until you choose another day.
- **What to list.** *Expired*, *Expiring* or *Expired and expiring*.
- **Look ahead.** How many days after the date counts as expiring. Empty takes the business's own setting (30 days unless changed in the settings).
- **Place and item group.** Keep only what one place holds, or one group's items (its sub-groups included).

- **Print the report** opens the report engine: quick view, save or share. The report is the `stock_expiry` document: edit its layout like every other report in the report designer; the words and the data fields it offers are listed there.
- A list longer than 2,000 lines is cut and says so: narrow the date, the place or the group. Reading the stock is enough to see and print it.

Goods loaded for another site say from which place to which. What left to a customer or a loss, or came back, is shown apart with the movement that moved it. The pieces of an item tracked by piece are listed with their place, their state and their tags.

### Stock value and where goods came from

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to see what the stock on hand is worth, and where a delivery came from and whether it was paid.

Open **Stock → Stock value** (it needs the right to manage purchases or to read the finances). The page shows the **known cost** of what is on hand, as of a date, with when it was calculated and from how many movements. It is an *operational value*: the recorded movements at the cost recorded for each lot. It is not a posted stock-book balance and it states no cost of goods.

Goods whose cost is **unknown** (a lot received with no cost, or goods with no lot) are listed by quantity and are **not** in the total; the page says *Not complete* while any exist, and a known cost of zero is a priced line, not an unknown one. A movement recorded later with an earlier date changes a later calculation, never the facts recorded before.

Open any movement (**Stock → Movements**) and read **Where this came from**: the receipt, the order, the supplier bills linked to that order, what each bill owes and the payments and reversals that settle it. Only what is recorded is shown; nothing is matched on a name, a date or an amount. A part you may not see says *not shown to you* (that is not the same as unpaid); a part with nothing recorded says *not linked*; a list cut short says so. Linking a bill never changes what the goods cost. Opening it changes nothing.

<!-- anchor: user.money.stock.reversals -->
### Returns, corrections and counts

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to take goods back, undo a movement, or put right a figure that is wrong on the shelf, without editing history.

Open a movement in **Stock → Movements**. Where the feature *Returns and corrections* is on and your role may move stock, the page says how much of that movement can still be reversed and offers: **Return from the customer** (for a sale), **Return to the supplier** (for a delivery) or **Correct this movement** (for the others). The form shows what moved and what remains before you confirm. A return of goods to the supplier leaves from the place the goods are in now; goods back from a customer return to the place you choose. A correction is a movement back, and a correction can itself be corrected.

**Counts.** Where the feature *Stock counts* is on, **Stock → Stock counts** lists what was counted against what the ledger held. **Count** records an item at a place and moves nothing: the count waits for someone who may approve adjustments, and says so. The approval applies exactly the difference, and only if the stock has not moved since the count; otherwise it says the count must be done again. Whoever counted does not approve their own count while someone else may; the owner of a one-person business does both.

**Good to know**

- A return or a correction never exceeds what the movement it reverses allows, however many times it is sent.
- No payment, refund or credit is made by it: if money is involved, correct it in the invoice or the supplier payment.
- Sending the same form again, after a lost answer, records it once.

<!-- anchor: user.money.stock.cycle -->
### Cycle counts

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You want to check parts of the stock on a schedule while the shop stays open, without a full stocktake.

Open **Stock → Cycle counts** (the features *Stock counts* and *Cycle counts* must be on). It has up to three tabs.

- **To count** lists the tasks. Anyone with the right to count (or who manages the stock) opens a task and counts it **blind**: each line says what to count and where, and never what the ledger expects, so the figure cannot sway the count. Scan or type a barcode to find the line, then **Count** it and enter what you found, or **Skip** it with a reason. A person who may only count is told the line is counted and nothing more; someone who manages the stock is told the difference. The task is complete when every line is counted or skipped.
- **Plans** (for who manages the stock) say what to count and when: *Places* (with everything inside them), *Items*, or *High value* (goods worth at least a threshold, yours or the business's), once or every n days from a due day. When a plan is due it makes one task listing its lines; the same day never makes two.
- **History** says, for each item at each place, when it was last counted and how many counts were exact.

A counted line is a count of *Stock counts*: it waits for someone who may approve adjustments, and only an approved count changes a balance, applying exactly its difference and only if the stock has not moved since. A task still open after the business's overdue days raises one notice in the inbox, which opens this page; it ends when the task is complete.

### How amounts are shown

**Audience:** Everyone

You want to read a figure without second-guessing what it includes.

**Good to know**

- Amounts use your workspace's currency and your number format. An invoice keeps the currency it was issued in.
- On the statement, charges carry a minus sign and payments and credits a plus. A balance in red is money you owe.
- Where a price includes VAT it says so, for example "incl. VAT 20 %". The invoice PDF lists the VAT it contains.
- If your workspace does not charge VAT, no VAT is shown.
- Days are shown in whole and half days.

### Invoices a business made out to you as a customer

**Audience:** Member

A business that invoices you can give you a code, so you read what it invoices you from your own account, without joining it.

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money) and, under **Outstanding**, choose **Use a billing code**.
2. Enter the code and choose **Check the code**. You see which business invites you and in whose name its invoices are made out.
3. Choose **Accept**. The invoices appear under **Billed to you as a customer**.

**Good to know**

- One card per business and per debtor: a person buying personally and for a company has two cards, and two debtors of one business are never added together. Amounts of different currencies are never added either.
- These invoices are not counted with what you owe as a member, and they are not in your member lists or exports.
- A code that cannot be used (wrong, expired, withdrawn, or already taken by another account) always gets the same answer: ask the business for a new one.
- Accepting makes you no member of the business. The business can end the access, and so can you; nothing is erased when it ends.
- Paying these invoices from here is not offered yet.

**See also:** [Your finances across spaces](help:user.money.finances)

### Paying invoices without a month

**Audience:** Member

A business issued you invoices that belong to no month. They are paid together, on their own.

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money) and tick the invoices without a month you want to pay. They belong to one business and one currency.
2. A bar shows how many you ticked and their total. Choose **Pay** to pay online, or **I have paid** if you already paid another way.
3. The confirmation lists the invoices and one total. Confirm: one checkout opens, or one declaration is sent.

**Good to know**

- Invoices without a month and monthly invoices are never paid together: once you tick one kind, the other cannot be ticked, and a line says so. If the business refuses a mix anyway, you are told so and nothing is charged.
- The amount is what the invoices still owe. If it changed, refresh and try again.
- If the answer was lost on the way, choose **Pay** again: it is the same payment, found again, never a second one. A payment is shown as paid only once the business has received it.
- The money is posted in the month it arrives. What an invoice does not take stays your credit with that business until it is allocated.
- A business whose payment service is too old says so before anything is sent.

**See also:** [Your finances across spaces](help:user.money.finances)

### Your businesses at a glance

**Audience:** Administrator · Owner

You run or manage one or more businesses and want to see, from Me, what each is still owed and what each has to pay.

**Steps**

1. Open [My finances](app:/money). If you may read the finances of a business, a switch at the top offers **Personal** and **My businesses**.
2. Choose **My businesses**. Each business has a card with one block per currency: **To collect**, **Declared, awaiting confirmation**, **Received, not yet matched** and **Awaiting approval**.
3. Tap a figure to see the invoices behind it, **Show more** when there are more, and tap an invoice to open it in its business.
4. Under **To pay**, **Suppliers** and **Expenses owed to members** are listed apart; tap either to see the obligations behind it.

**Good to know**

- Currencies are never added together, and nothing is set against what the business owes. A refund due to customers (**Refunds due to customers**) is a line of its own.
- A held payable is part of what remains, not an addition to it.
- A business that could not be read says so, with **Try again**; that is not the same as nothing being owed. A business whose finances you may not read does not appear.
- Being a customer of a business gives you no access to its figures. Opening a row only takes you to the business: what you may do there is decided by your role in it.

**See also:** [Your finances across spaces](help:user.money.finances)
